Markets can look like an impenetrable wall of jargon and blinking numbers. They
are not. Underneath, a small number of ideas explain most of what you see.

## Prices are opinions, settled in cash

Every price is the point where a buyer and a seller disagree just enough to
trade. When you read that a stock "fell 3%", what changed is not the company —
it's the collective opinion about the company's future, expressed in money.

## The three questions we ask about any asset

1. **What do you own?** A share of a business, a loan to a government, a claim
   on a commodity?
2. **What could go wrong?** Every return is compensation for a risk. Name it.
3. **What is already priced in?** The news everyone knows is usually in the
   price. Edge comes from what the crowd underweights.

## Start small, write things down

The members who improve fastest are the ones who **write**. Pick one company,
form a view, and defend it in a page. Six months later, read it back. That loop —
thesis, time, review — is the whole game in miniature.

> Curiosity compounds. So does the habit of writing your reasoning down.

Welcome to the club.
