Every quarter, listed companies publish results. For a beginner the document
looks endless. It isn't — if you know where to look.

## The four numbers that anchor everything

- **Revenue** — is the business growing, and how fast versus last year?
- **Margins** — of each euro of sales, how much survives as profit?
- **Cash flow** — profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. Do they match?
- **Guidance** — what does management expect next? Markets trade the future.

## Then read the story

Numbers without narrative mislead. After the figures, read management's
commentary and ask: *does the story explain the numbers, or excuse them?* A
company can beat estimates and still fall if its guidance disappoints — because
price reflects expectations, not history.

## A 15-minute routine

1. Skim the headline figures (2 min).
2. Compare to what the market expected (3 min).
3. Read the guidance and one paragraph of commentary (5 min).
4. Write two sentences: what changed, and what it means (5 min).

Do this ten times and reports stop being intimidating. Do it fifty times and you
start to notice what management *isn't* saying.
